How Muse powers it

Muse is the layer between the posts and the person.

On one side: informal Group posts with no fields at all. On the other: a person with a need, written in one sentence. Neither side is structured. Muse translates both into the same schema, and only then can they be compared.

"But Facebook already has filters." We tested them.

Marketplace lets you filter by location and maximum price. So we gave filters a head start, with perfect prices and locations, and tried a common case: two friends, South Lake Union, June 1 to August 31, with $3,000 a month between them.

34results

  • 7 miss the dates. There is no filter for "my stay".
  • 5 are per-person prices that cost over $3,000 for two.
Filter · max $1,500 each
23results
  • 4 homes that fit are hidden. Whole apartments at $2,196 to $2,832 look "too expensive".

Nest · with Muse
25homes that fit
  • 21 rooms and 4 whole apartments, each compared on what two people would actually pay.
  • All cover the full stay. 3 more are shown, labelled as part of the stay.

A filter compares one number to one number. It does not know if $2,400 is for a room or the whole apartment, or whether "July 1" covers your summer. With filters you pick which mistake to make. Muse reads what the price and dates mean, so Nest does not have to pick.

Roommates are where a filter gives up

A home has a price and a date. A person has habits, and people describe habits in their own words. We ran one request (South Lake Union, June 1 to August 31, up to $1,500 per person) against every roommate post.

Seven jobs Muse does in Nest

01

Classify every post

Into 10 categories, from housing listing and lease takeover to roommate request, furniture, and events, so each post is routed to the right extractor.

02

Extract housing

19 typed fields through structured outputs against a strict JSON schema: rent, rent basis, city, neighborhood, coordinates, dates, type, amenities.

03

Look at the photos

Two-pass and multimodal. Text first at minimal reasoning effort; images only when fields are missing. The second pass fills gaps and never overrides text.

04

Build roommate profiles

A second-stage extraction on roommate posts: cleanliness, sleep, smoking, pets, social style as fixed enums, each backed by a verbatim quote checked against the post.

05

Understand the request

One sentence becomes a 13-field search goal: intent, city, neighborhoods, ISO dates, budget, basis, home type, preferences.

06

Resolve places

The prompt carries a live catalog of cities and neighborhoods from our database, so "SLU" and "downtown" resolve to areas that have listings.

07

Ask when it matters

If the city, dates, or budget are missing, Muse returns one short follow-up question. Otherwise it stays out of the way.

Guardrails

Never guess

Null over invention, schema validation before every write, duplicate detection across groups, retries with backoff, a mock adapter fallback, and the key kept server-side.

Where AI helped most

Executing a plan we had already made

  • Building screens and pipeline code from one shared brief.
  • Turning every real failure into a test.
Where it fell short

Guessing and latency

  • Muse invented a neighborhood. We now validate against the database.
  • Muse-written explanations added 8 seconds. We moved them into code.
